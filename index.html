Multi-omics Network Analytics · Technical University of Denmark

Learning the structure of biology

We build AI that learns from biological networks. By combining multi-omics data, knowledge graphs and graph representation learning, we turn heterogeneous measurements into predictive and interpretable models of microbial communities, host–microbe interactions and human disease.

Our vision

Biology is relational: function emerges from interactions between molecules, cells, organisms and their environment. Yet most computational models still treat measurements as independent features. We believe the next generation of biomedical AI will learn directly from this relational structure, representing proteins, microbes, patients and prior knowledge in a shared space where every prediction can be traced back to a mechanism.

Our goal is AI that is multimodal by construction, interpretable by design and grounded in FAIR, machine-actionable data, so that discoveries can move from the computer to the clinic and the biofoundry.

Research themes

Graph representation learning & explainable AI

We train graph neural networks on individual-specific microbial association networks and interpret what drives their predictions. In inflammatory bowel disease, this revealed a densely connected module of co-abundant Bacteroidales whose associations, rather than abundance shifts, carry the predictive signal.

IBD graph neural networks

Knowledge graphs & semantic data

From the Clinical Knowledge Graph, with close to 20 million nodes and 220 million relationships linking clinical proteomics to biomedical knowledge, to the PREFER ontology for precision fermentation, we build the structured knowledge that AI needs to reason over biology.

Clinical Knowledge Graph · PREFER · Graph data science in fungal biotechnology

Generative & deep learning models for omics

ABaCo combines a variational autoencoder with an adversarial discriminator to integrate metagenomic studies, correcting technical heterogeneity while preserving biological signal. InstaNexus couples AI-driven de novo peptide sequencing with assembly to reconstruct full-length antibodies and nanobodies directly from mass spectrometry data.

ABaCo · InstaNexus

Microbiomes, pathogens & host interactions

We study how microbes adapt and interact with their hosts: comparative genomics of 66 clinically relevant skin fungi, host–parasite protein interaction predictions across 46 parasites and four hosts, and statistical frameworks to find candidate genetic interactions in adaptive laboratory evolution.

Skin fungi genomics · OrthoHPI 2.0 · ALE genetic interactions

AI-ready data & autonomous laboratories

Models are only as good as their data. We argue for data-centric AI, built on curated repositories, consistent metadata and semantic standards, as the foundation for autonomous virtual laboratories, and we build the tooling that makes it practical.

Beyond the model · MGni.py · VueGen

Open science & training

Our software, data and course materials are open. We teach multi-omics data analysis, protein language models, AI-guided protein science and network approaches to microbiology, so that these methods reach the people who generate the data.

Courses · GitHub

Where we are heading

  • Multimodal foundation models for biology. Joint representations of sequence, structure, abundance, interaction networks and clinical context that transfer across cohorts, species and tasks.
  • Explainable graph AI as a discovery engine. From accurate predictions to testable hypotheses about interacting modules, pathways and mechanisms.
  • Knowledge graphs meet large language models. Grounding generative AI in curated biomedical knowledge for retrieval, reasoning and literature-scale curation.
  • Agentic, data-centric science. AI agents working on FAIR, machine-actionable data to close the design–build–test–learn loop in biofoundries and translational research.
  • Clinical translation. Microbiome and proteomics models that stratify patients and point towards interventions.
